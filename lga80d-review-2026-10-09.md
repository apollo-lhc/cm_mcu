# LGA80D usage review against the datasheets (2026-10-09)

I went through every firmware path that touches the LGA80Ds and checked each against the ZL8802
datasheet, the LGA80D TRN 3.3 and the Rev3 schematic. Most of the PMBus timing is now within the
datasheet limits. The real gaps are in power sequencing, in checking that the init settings
actually took effect, and in which temperature sensor the alarm uses. No code was changed and
nothing was built.

Updated later on 2026-10-09: results of the cheap checks (run with `cm_interface/lga80d_checks.py`)
are in the last section, and the PowerSupplyTask period is corrected from 25 ms to 20 ms
(`pdMS_TO_TICKS(25)` truncates to 2 ticks at the 100 Hz tick rate). Three statements in the
first version were wrong and are fixed below: the power-good margin, the 3V3/1V8 turn-on stagger,
and the claim in #1 that a loop overrun leaves VCCINT's EN low for close to 0 ms (it stays low at
least until VCCINT's own PG drops). #7 now explains that the MonitorTask error paths stay on the
same module only for page-0 failures.

## a) What I looked at

**Firmware (REV3 paths, plus a partial pass over `prod_test`)**
- `LocalTasks.c`: `LGA80D_init`, `snapdump`/`snapreset`, the device and command tables.
- `MonitorTask.c` and the `lga80d_settle()` helper in `Tasks.h`.
- `I2CCommunication.c` (`apollo_pmbus_rw`, timeouts); `common/smbus.c` (bus speed, SMBus
  timeout, PEC); `common/smbus_units.c` (Linear11/Linear16 conversions).
- `ProgComTask.c` (the DC and SN paths); `commands/PowerCommands.c` (`psreg`, `snapshot`,
  `sn_all`); `MCU_Reg.c` (power inhibit and fault clear).
- `PowerSupplyTask.c` and `common/power_ctl.c` (enable/power-good sequencing).
- `AlarmUtilities.c` and `I2CSlaveTask.c` (the consumers of the LGA80D temperatures).
- `prod_test/PowerI2CCommands.c`. Added 2026-10-10: `prod_test/I2CCommunication.c` (its own
  polling wrappers, not the cm_mcu interrupt path) and `prod_test/commands.c` (`disable_ps`
  callers). The rest of `prod_test` was not reviewed.

**Documents**
- ZL8802 datasheet FN8760 Rev 3.00 (the controller inside the module).
- LGA80D TRN Release 3.3 (Jan 2026). I did not re-read the Rev 2.6 app note; 3.3 replaces it.
- Schematic 6089-127 RevA, sheets 3.02–3.06 (PDF pages 27–31): the SYNC/CFG/ADDR straps,
  EN/PG wiring, SALRT and SHARE pins.

I took the `-0.50 C` glitch and settle-delay history from earlier notes and did not
re-investigate it.

**Hardware straps against the TRN: all OK**
- Master address 0x44 is slave + 1, which the TRN requires for 4-phase.
- CFG 110k/121k selects 4-phase master/slave at 45 A.
- 3V3/1V8 SYNC is 38.3k, i.e. 800 kHz. The TRN requires at least 533 kHz for outputs from 2.5 to
  3.3 V, so this is fine.
- The GTY modules' SYNC is 23.7k, i.e. 457 kHz.
- The VCCINT SYNC resistor is deliberately left off, on Artesyn's advice. Those modules therefore
  boot at the open-pin 400 kHz until the firmware writes 457 kHz.
- SALRT is unconnected on every module.
- The YAML page naming matches the TRN (page 1 = Vout1).

## b) Issues found (most important first)

### 1. Nothing enforces a minimum off-time before the supplies are re-enabled
- **What the firmware does:** `disable_ps()` returns as soon as power-good (PG) is low. The next
  `POWER_OFF` pass re-enables `EN_F*_INT` if the off request has cleared.
  - PowerSupplyTask handles only one queue message per pass (`PowerSupplyTask.c:200`). A pass is
    20 ms: the code asks for `pdMS_TO_TICKS(25)` (`PowerSupplyTask.c:607`), which truncates to 2
    ticks at 100 Hz. A ProgCom "assert inhibit / release inhibit" pair, or "clear power fault"
    right after a failure, therefore re-enables within one or two passes.
  - `vTaskDelayUntil` (`PowerSupplyTask.c:607`) does not sleep once it has fallen behind, and
    `disable_ps()` busy-waits (`power_ctl.c:155`). After an overrun, the next passes run
    back-to-back.
- **What the datasheet says:**
  - The EN pin is edge-triggered and has minimum pulse widths (ZL8802 p.16, Eq. 1/2): EN low ≥
    TOFF_DELAY + TOFF_FALL + 10.5 ms, and EN high ≥ TON_DELAY + TON_RISE + PG_DELAY + 5.5 ms.
    That is about 20 ms with default settings. Shorter pulses "may result in the device not
    responding to the trailing edge."
  - PG drops only when VOUT falls below VOUT_UV_FAULT_LIMIT (default 0.85 × VSET, about 0.72 V
    for VCCINT). In 4-phase mode TOFF_FALL is ignored and the FETs are tri-stated, so how fast
    the output decays depends on the load.
  - There is no prebias protection in 4-phase current sharing (TRN p.13, ZL8802 p.16).
- **Possible consequences:** VCCINT is disabled last, and `disable_ps()` then waits for its own
  PG to drop before returning. Its EN therefore stays low from that point until the next pass,
  and which datasheet limit breaks depends on how fast PG drops:
  - **Loaded rail, PG drops fast:** EN low ≈ 20 ms minus the time the higher-priority rails take
    to drop PG. With the register values read on 2026-10-09, Eq. 1 gives 20.5 ms, so this is too
    short even if PG drops instantly. If immediate-off (ON_OFF_CONFIG bit 0, set on every module)
    removes TOFF_FALL from Eq. 1, the minimum is 15.5 ms and is missed once the higher-priority
    rails take more than 4.5 ms; the datasheet doesn't say which applies.
  - **Unloaded rail, PG drops slowly:** EN stays low at least as long as VCCINT takes to fall
    below 0.72 V, which is probably enough for Eq. 1. But the rail is re-enabled shortly after
    that, either on the next pass or straight away if the wait overran the loop, so it restarts
    with roughly 0.72 V still on it. 4-phase rails have no prebias protection. A board with no
    FPGA load, like CM3001, is in this case.
  - After an overrun, the L1ON check reads PG microseconds after EN goes high. That gives a
    spurious `POWER_FAILURE` and an EN-high pulse far below Eq. 2, so the module may miss the
    falling edge and stay on (see #5).
- **Known since the 2026-10-09 run:** TOFF_DELAY and TOFF_FALL are 5 ms and ON_OFF_CONFIG is
  0x17 (immediate off) on every module.
- **Not known:** the real PG fall times, and whether any host script does a quick off/on.
  Nothing shows this happened on CM3001. It is one more candidate to compare against the FA's
  overcurrent at enable.

### 2. Only `LGA80D_init` sets VCCINT to 457 kHz, and nothing checks that the writes took effect
- **Result 2026-10-09:** on the tested board the writes took effect (VCCINT measured
  456.5–459 kHz, droop and gain as written, both pairs consistent). This lowers the priority of
  this finding; what remains is the failure path below.
- The writes go out once per MCU boot and are volatile (no STORE).
- Failures are logged as `LGA80D(n)` without device or page, and power-up continues anyway
  (`LocalTasks.c:565-586`). A failed PAGE write sends the following writes to the wrong page
  (already known).
- An ACK does not mean the value was applied. The device ACKs writes with invalid data, or to
  protected commands, and only flags STATUS_CML bit 6 or bit 1. The 10-02 test showed CML clean
  after init, so protection is not a problem today.
- A partly applied init can leave master and slave on one shared SYNC/DDC rail with different
  frequencies (457 vs 400 kHz) or different droop, and the rail is enabled anyway. The datasheet
  wants the configuration consistent across a 4-phase group, and FREQUENCY_SWITCH "set as close
  as possible" to the external clock.
- The datasheet's summary says the only sure way to apply a setting is STORE_USER_ALL plus a
  power cycle. I have not confirmed that FREQUENCY_SWITCH takes effect at the next enable.
- A module-only reset (a VIN brownout the MCU rides through) would silently revert these
  settings.
- `prod_test` never calls `LGA80D_init`, so production test powers VCCINT at 400 kHz, with the
  strapped droop and ramp gain 3 — not the operating configuration.

### 3. The temperature alarm and the IPMC value use the controller-die sensor, not the module hotspot
- `AlarmUtilities.c:168` and `I2CSlaveTask.c:111` (the IPMC's "hottest regulator" value) use
  command index 0, which is READ_TEMPERATURE_1 (0x8D, controller die).
- The TRN says the hotspot is the MOSFET, and READ_TEMPERATURE_3 (0x8F, paged) is the DrMOS
  temperature. 0x8F is read on every loop but feeds nothing.
- The module still protects itself (125 °C fault and 110 °C warning on the defaults). But the
  system alarm (default 70 °C) and the IPMC number read low by the die-to-DrMOS difference. I
  don't know how big that difference is on this board.
- **Result 2026-10-09:** not answered. The rails were unloaded during the run, and at idle the
  two sensors agree within a few °C. It needs a run with configured FPGAs drawing current.

### 4. ProgCom DC writes are an unrestricted passthrough
- `ProgComTask.c:377` accepts any register, any page, at any time, regardless of rail state.
- The datasheet says to disable the device before writing settings. The exceptions are
  OPERATION, ON_OFF_CONFIG, CLEAR_FAULTS, VOUT_COMMAND and the margin commands.
  FREQUENCY_SWITCH says explicitly "output must be disabled."
- A single-page VOUT_COMMAND or OPERATION write to one module of a 4-phase pair breaks the
  group's consistency. This is FA hypothesis C again.
- ProgCom writes require at least one data byte (`ProgComTask.c:259`), so CLEAR_FAULTS and
  STORE_* (send-byte commands) cannot be sent cleanly from the host.

### 5. `disable_ps()` waits on PG with no timeout and no yield
- `power_ctl.c:155` is a tight loop at priority +4.
- PG falls only when VOUT drops below the UV limit. On an unloaded VCCINT rail that can take a
  long time, and the +3 tasks (MonitorTask, CLI, ProgCom) are starved meanwhile.
- A PG that never drops hangs the task with no log. That could be back-feed from another rail,
  or a module that missed its EN edge as in #1. The hardware watchdog is not armed.
- `prod_test` calls the same `disable_ps()` from `power_off`, `restart` and at the end of
  `prodtest1` (`prod_test/commands.c:99, 107, 200`), so a PG that stays high hangs its CLI
  task. Production test runs on unproven boards, where that is more likely.

### 6. Diagnostic data goes stale by design
- **Latched faults:** CLEAR_FAULTS is never sent. The datasheet says fault bits clear only on
  CLEAR_FAULTS, so STATUS_WORD in `psmon` and ZynqMon shows any fault since the last 12 V
  cycle. `PS_ANYFAIL_ALARM_CLEAR` does not clear the modules. An old IOUT_OC cannot be told
  apart from a new one.
  - Caveat: the 10-04 soak saw a CML bit re-set after a clear that was never logged, so this
    behaviour is not fully understood.
- **SNAPSHOT:** after a fault the snapshot is written to NVRAM once. It is not updated again
  until SNAPSHOT_CONTROL = 3 (erase) is written, and the erase only works with the output off
  (ZL8802 0xF3).
  - Nothing erases at a safe moment, such as in `POWER_OFF`.
  - `snapshot` may therefore show the first fault since the last erase, not the latest one. That
    matters for the FA.

### 7. Remaining PMBus spacing gaps
The rules are 2 ms between reads, 5 ms between other commands to the same device, and 100 ms
quiet after STORE/RESTORE.

These paths still miss it:
- **MonitorTask error paths:** `continue` after a failed PAGE write (`MonitorTask.c:126`) and
  `break` after a failed read (`MonitorTask.c:161`) only leave the current page, not the module.
  - A failure on page 1 falls through to the next supply, a different module, so there is no
    spacing problem.
  - A failure on page 0 is followed within about 1 ms by the PAGE=1 write to the same module.
  - The impact is small. It only follows a failure, and the next monitor cycle retries about
    1 s later. The likely cost is one bad page-1 sample in that cycle; only READ_TEMPERATURE_1
    is range-checked.
  - If the failed transaction was cut off mid-command rather than NACKed at the address, the
    datasheet's warning that incomplete commands can halt PMBus communication also applies.
  - The comment at `MonitorTask.c:156` ("abort reading this device") is wrong for the same
    reason: the `break` only abandons the current page.
  - *Fixed in the working tree (2026-10-10): `lga80d_settle()` on both error paths for page 0
    (PSMON only), and the comment corrected. A settle does not recover a module halted by a
    cut-off transaction; the next cycle would just fail again.*
- **`snapshot <n> 1`:** the PAGE write for the reset follows the block read within about 1 ms
  (`PowerCommands.c:304`). *Fixed in the working tree (2026-10-10): `lga80d_settle()` before
  `snapreset`.*
- **`sn_all`:** the erase write is followed by the next PAGE write within about 1 ms. The erase
  is a flash operation whose duration is not specified. *Fixed in the working tree
  (2026-10-10): `lga80d_settle()` after the page-0 erase. The erase time is still unspecified,
  so the 10–20 ms settle is not a guaranteed figure.*
  - Residual for both: the settle runs with `i2c1_sem` released, so MonitorTask can reach the
    same module within about 1 ms. This is rare, and it predates the fix.
- **ProgCom back-to-back commands:** the gap is one UART round trip, probably under 5 ms.
- **`prod_test`:** after a PAGE write the next command follows after 0–10 ms (the one-tick
  `vTaskDelay` poll in `prod_test/I2CCommunication.c`), so below 5 ms about half the time
  (`prod_test/PowerI2CCommands.c:100-121, 228-240`). Same pattern as the member-module glitch.
  The risk is low: about 21 PAGE→command pairs per run, and a glitch gives a false FAIL, not a
  false PASS.

### Minor
- `LGA80D_init` also writes the AVTT/CC modules (devices 5 and 6, 2-output). The frequency
  matches their strap, ramp gain is ignored in 2-output mode, and the droop adds about 2 mV at
  30 A. It is harmless but unintended: the comment says "all devices except the 0th", and
  devices 5 and 6 arrived with REV2.
- `psreg` always reads 2 bytes, and ProgCom defaults to 1 byte. Whether the ZL8802 flags a
  length mismatch as CML bit 1 ("other communication fault") is not documented. If it does,
  these tools contaminate the CML evidence from the glitch investigation.
- PowerSupplyTask's period is 20 ms, not the 25 ms its code asks for and its comments state
  (`PowerSupplyTask.c:607` and the MC snapshot comment above it): `pdMS_TO_TICKS(25)` truncates
  to 2 ticks at `configTICK_RATE_HZ = 100`.

### Checked, no issue
- **Bus settings:** I2C1 at 100 kHz, 25 ms SMBus timeout, PEC off (optional).
- **Data formats:** the VOUT exponent of −13 matches VOUT_MODE 0x13.
- **Init values:** 457.14 kHz gives N = 35, which is valid; droop and gain are in range; inrush
  at gain 7 is about 5 A into roughly 9 mF.
- **Power-good timing (estimated, not measured):** PG is checked about 20 ms after EN (one
  PowerSupplyTask pass). From the register values read on 2026-10-09, PG is expected about 13 ms
  after EN on the 2-output modules and about 9.6 ms on VCCINT, leaving roughly 7 ms and 10 ms.
  The TRN's 0–2 ms turn-on delay tolerance can reduce the 2-output margin to about 5 ms. The
  first version of this report said 25 ms and an 18 ms TRN worst case; both numbers were wrong.
  A scope measurement of EN against PG at turn-on would settle it.
- **Enable rules:** both 3V3/1V8 enables rise together. The first version said the TRN's default
  turn-on stagger (EN1 5 ms, EN2 10 ms) handles that; the modules actually read TON_DELAY 5 ms on
  both pages, so there is no stagger. The ZL8802 rule (no EN edge within 1 ms of the other
  channel's ramp start) is still met, because both edges are at t≈0 and both ramps start about
  5 ms later. Per the TRN, the order in which 3V3 and 1V8 come up is not guaranteed. AVCC and
  AVTT are enabled one 20 ms pass apart.
- **Init with outputs off:** guaranteed by hardware (GPIO tri-state plus pulldowns, and EN held
  low through the boot-loader window), not checked by firmware.

## Datasheet requirements we could run into

| Requirement | Source | Firmware status |
|---|---|---|
| Disable the device before writing settings; FREQUENCY_SWITCH needs output off | ZL8802 PMBus Use Guidelines, 0x33 | Init: OK by hardware. ProgCom: not enforced (#4) |
| 2 ms between reads, 5 ms between other commands, 100 ms after STORE/RESTORE | ZL8802 guidelines; TRN | Mostly OK; gaps in #7 |
| EN low/high minimum widths (Eq. 1/2) | ZL8802 p.16 | Not enforced (#1) |
| No prebias protection in 4-phase | TRN p.13; ZL8802 p.16 | No discharge wait (#1) |
| 2-channel: no EN edge within ±1 ms of the other channel's ramp start | ZL8802 p.16 | OK |
| EN low > 110 ms at power-up; VIN above UVLO before EN | TRN p.110–111 | OK via boot loader; VIN not checked |
| 4-phase group configured consistently; SYNC/DDC tied; master address = slave + 1 | ZL8802 p.20, 0x37; TRN p.109 | Hardware OK; init consistency not verified (#2) |
| FREQUENCY_SWITCH 400–800 kHz (≥ 533 kHz for 2.5–3.3 V) | TRN Table 3 and p.108 | OK |
| Fault bits latch until CLEAR_FAULTS | ZL8802 fault-response tables | Never cleared (#6) |
| SNAPSHOT captures one fault until erased; erase only when disabled | ZL8802 0xF3 | No erase policy (#6) |
| Incomplete PMBus commands can halt the interface; recover by disable/re-enable | ZL8802 SMBus section | No detection; an MCU reset drops EN, which recovers it |
| Module hotspot is the MOSFET (0x8F) | TRN OT protection, 0x8F | Alarm uses 0x8D (#3) |

## Cheap checks to settle the open points
The first three are scripted in `cm_interface/lga80d_checks.py` (`--check freq|temp|timing`,
read-only).
- **Did the frequency write take?** With VCCINT on, run `psreg 10 95`, `11 95`, `20 95`,
  `21 95`, `30 95`, `31 95`, `40 95`, `41 95` (READ_FREQUENCY). About 457 kHz means it took
  effect; 400 kHz means it did not.
- **How big is the die-to-hotspot gap?** Compare `psmon 0` with `psmon 1` under FPGA load.
- **What are the real delays?** Read 0x60, 0x61, 0x64, 0x65, 0xD4 and 0x02 on a VCCINT and a GTY
  module to get the actual turn-on/off and power-good delays.
- **How long do PG rise and fall take?** Scope EN against PG at turn-on (the real margin to the
  20 ms check) and at turn-off (how long `disable_ps()` waits), on a VCCINT rail and a 2-output
  rail, loaded and unloaded.

## Results of the cheap checks (2026-10-09)
One run of `lga80d_checks.py` on the board at lnx5186 `/dev/ttyUSB2` (board ID not recorded),
MCU power state `POWER_ON`. The rails were effectively unloaded: VCCINT and AVTT/CC `READ_IOUT`
between −3.4 and +0.2 A (cm_interface notes that unloaded outputs read negative). Only 1V8
carried real current (8 A).

- **freq:** `LGA80D_init` took effect. VCCINT measured 456.5–459 kHz (FREQUENCY_SWITCH reads
  457.0), droop 0.0699 mV/A and ramp gain 7 on every module it writes, both 4-phase pairs
  consistent. 3V3/1V8 is untouched at 800 kHz, droop 0, gain 3. AVTT/CC also carries droop
  0.0699 and gain 7 (the minor note above). VOUT_COMMAND is 0.8500 V on all eight VCCINT phases;
  it is volatile, so this says nothing about the FA board's history.
- **temp:** not answered (unloaded). The two sensors agree within a few °C, which is expected
  with no DrMOS dissipation; the hottest module was 3V3/1V8 at about 41 °C on both.
- **timing:** every module is at the defaults: TON_DELAY, TON_RISE, TOFF_DELAY and TOFF_FALL
  5 ms, POWER_GOOD_DELAY 1 ms, ON_OFF_CONFIG 0x17 (EN pin only, immediate off), POWER_GOOD_ON 90%
  and VOUT_UV_FAULT_LIMIT 85% of VOUT_COMMAND (VCCINT PG drops below 0.722 V). DDC_CONFIG matches
  the expected phase layout (0x44 = phases 0/2, 0x43 = phases 1/3, rail 4). Eq. 1/2 give EN low
  ≥ 20.5 ms and EN high ≥ 16.5 ms. Page mapping matches the TRN (page 1 = Vout1: 3V3, AVCC).

What this changes:
- #2 drops in priority: the writes work; the remaining issue is the failure path.
- #1 and #5 depend on load, in different ways. Immediate-off tri-states the FETs, so PG falls
  quickly on a loaded rail and slowly on an unloaded one, where VCCINT decays from 0.85 V to
  0.72 V only through leakage.
  - Loaded boards are where the EN-low time can fall short of Eq. 1.
  - Unloaded boards (this one, CM3001) are the worst case for the `disable_ps()` wait, the loop
    overrun and the residual voltage at re-enable.
  - The scope measurement is needed on both.
- #3 stays open until a loaded run.
